### The Three Laws of Lasting Infrastructure
Rome's roads lasted 2300+ years because they followed principles that transcend empire:

**1. Infrastructure Serves Multiple Masters**
Roman roads weren't just for legions - merchants, messengers, farmers, travelers all used them. Multi-purpose infrastructure outlasts single-purpose empires. Ethereum isn't just DeFi - it's NFTs, DAOs, gaming, identity, social. When one use case crashes, others persist. CEXs serve one master (shareholders). Protocols serve everyone.

**2. Infrastructure Has No Single Point of Failure**
Roman roads connected hundreds of cities. One blockage didn't collapse network. Distributed systems survive single-point failures. Uniswap runs on thousands of nodes. Aave operates across multiple chains. CEXs have one server, one company, one CEO. When that fails, empire burns.

**3. Infrastructure Costs More to Remove Than Ignore**
Tearing up Roman roads costs more than leaving them standing. Shutting down Ethereum costs more than ignoring it (attack every validator, every node, every fork). CEXs are expensive to maintain (servers, staff, compliance). When maintenance exceeds value, empires collapse. When removal exceeds benefit, infrastructure survives.

Blog post makes these laws explicit. Tool shows them quantified. Users learn framework, not just "protocols good, CEXs bad."
